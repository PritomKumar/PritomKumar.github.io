Research Focus

The permission problem in agentic AI

AI coding assistants like Claude Code and Cursor can now act autonomously — editing files, running commands, even deleting things. But they almost always ask for approval the same way, no matter what's at stake. A trivial formatting change and a destructive deletion look identical on screen. Decades of security-warning research tell us exactly what that produces: people learn to click through without reading, right when it matters most.

Starting from interviews with developers who use these tools daily, I designed a notification that signals an action's actual risk — instead of showing nothing, or relying on a blunt rule like "every deletion is dangerous." I'm now running a study comparing that approach against those two cheaper alternatives, to see which one leads to better-calibrated approval decisions: catching what's risky, approving what's safe, without training people to tune out.

The goal is concrete design guidance for the next generation of agentic tools — not just whether warnings work, but which kind of signal earns the right amount of trust.
