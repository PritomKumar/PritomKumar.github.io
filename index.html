About

Engineer first, researcher now

I'm a doctoral researcher in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Helsinki, working on human–computer interaction. I come from Bangladesh, where I studied software engineering at the Institute of Information Technology, University of Dhaka. In 2022 I moved to Helsinki for a master's in data science, and since 2024 I have been doing my PhD with funding from Finland's national AI doctoral programme, FCAI AI-DOC.

My work sits between building and studying: I write the software that runs an experiment, then design the study that shows whether it works for people. That habit comes from software engineering, including an internship at SELISE, where I was part of a healthcare team of nine developers working in an agile, .NET environment.

I believe in the aggregation of marginal gains: a small improvement each day adds up to a significant result. It has guided me through my studies and my work. Outside the lab I read, draw and exercise, and I'd like to visit every continent.

Research

Designing trust between people and autonomous AI

The permission problem in agentic AI

Study in progress

AI coding assistants like Claude Code and Cursor can now act autonomously: editing files, running commands, even deleting things. But they almost always ask for approval the same way, no matter what's at stake. A trivial formatting change and a destructive deletion look identical on screen.

Decades of security-warning research tell us what that does: it trains people to click through without reading, right when it matters most.

  1. Interviews

    Developers who use agentic coding tools every day. This is the starting point for the design.

  2. A risk-signalling notification

    A notification that signals an action's actual risk, instead of showing nothing or relying on a cheap rule like “every deletion is dangerous”.

  3. A comparison study

    Three approaches, one question: which leads to better-calibrated approval decisions? Catching what's risky and approving what's safe, without training people to tune out.

The three approaches compared

  • AlternativeNo risk signal
  • AlternativeBlunt rule: every deletion is dangerous
  • My designCalibrated to actual risk

The goal is design guidance for the next generation of agentic tools: not just whether warnings work, but which kind of signal earns the right amount of trust.

    The risk levels are my own illustrative judgement for this demo. In the study, the design and the comparison are tested with real developers.

    Interactive demo (needs JavaScript). The same agent asks permission for six actions: renaming a file, deleting a build cache, editing a config file, overwriting a production settings file, force-pushing to main, and deleting exported data. Three designs are compared: the same prompt for everything; a blunt rule that flags every deletion; and a signal calibrated to actual risk. The blunt rule cries wolf about the harmless cache deletion and stays silent about the force-push.
